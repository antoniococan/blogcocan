## Introduction

*Algebraic geometry* is a branch of mathematics that combines techniques of (commutative) algebra with the language and the problems of geometry.

## Bézout’s Theorem

Let us discuss a classical result known as *Bézout’s Theorem* on intersection of curves. The phenomenon we try to investigate is the total number of intersection points of curves in the plane.

## Summary

:::theorem{title="Extreme Value Theorem"}

Every continuous function on a compact space is bounded.

:::

:::lemma{title="Boundedness of Convergent Sequences"}

Every convergent sequence is bounded.

:::

:::corollary{title="Boundedness"}

Every continuous function on a compact metric space is bounded.

:::

:::proposition{title="Uniqueness of Limits"}

A convergent sequence in a metric space has a unique limit.

:::

:::definition{title="Metric Space"}

A metric space is a set equipped with a distance function.

:::

:::example{title="The Real Numbers"}

The real numbers with the usual distance form a metric space.

:::

:::remark{title="Completeness"}

Not every metric space is complete.

:::
